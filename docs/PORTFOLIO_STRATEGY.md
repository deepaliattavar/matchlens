# Turn one focused project into a memorable portfolio story

## Your specific question

“They won the ball. What did they do with it?” Use this question consistently in the demo, repository opening, and portfolio introduction. The recognizable visual is two recovery sequences with different observed endings.

## What this project demonstrates today

- Data engineering: event validation, chronological processing, possession boundaries, reproducible sample, and evidence lineage.
- Analytics: a precise outcome definition; unresolved observations kept separate from completed outcomes.
- Communication: a visual comparison and an explanation that changes with the available evidence.
- AI judgment: human-reviewed prepared wording keeps inference out of the replay path, with honest provenance and fallback labels.

Connect these decisions to your own pipeline and analytics experience at Applied Materials when explaining the project. Describe work you understand and tested; disclose AI-assisted development honestly.

## What the synthetic examples cannot establish

The scenarios were authored to illustrate different outcomes. They cannot establish real team tendencies, player ability, causes of pressing success, or how common an outcome is. Do not present the contrast as a research finding about football.

## After the hackathon: a real-data companion study

Use a dataset whose license permits the intended analysis and publication. Before collecting or scraping data, verify permissions, event definitions and coverage. Define what counts as a recovery, where possession ends, how missing data is handled, and which outcomes are being compared. Decide whether a time horizon is appropriate and justify it.

Publish the selection rule and denominator, then inspect multiple sequences—not only examples that support an appealing story. Report pending or incomplete observations separately. Describe associations rather than claiming causal explanations from event data alone.

A possible article/video structure: question, data and selection rule, two traceable examples, aggregate results, alternative explanations, limitations. This is future work; no real-data results are included in this package.

## Tailor each application to the employer

Research the specific role, club or organization's audience, decisions and publicly documented tools. Use the job description and official technical material; mark unknowns rather than guessing the stack. Keep a short record:

| Research item | What to capture |
| --- | --- |
| User and decision | Who would use the output and what action it supports |
| Verified tools | Named platform/tool plus source URL and date |
| Relevant project evidence | A specific pipeline, validation, visualization or communication decision |
| Gap | What the employer needs that this prototype does not demonstrate |
| Tailored explanation | Two or three sentences connecting your work to that role |

For BI roles, lead with trustworthy transformations and audience design. For data engineering roles, lead with event contracts, state and lineage. For sports analytics roles, lead with the football question and limits of inference. Do not imply this broadcast prototype is already a validated coaching tool.

## Practical validation before making business claims

Ask a producer to trace and explain a set of sequences with and without the tool. Predefine measures such as factual errors, time to a usable card and editing effort. Report what is observed; do not invent savings for a pitch or résumé.
